Step 1: Immediate First-Aid (Do this today)

  • Prune bottom foliage: Clip off all leaves touching the soil or showing brown spots using clean pruning shears.
  • Sterilize tools: Wipe blades with 70% rubbing alcohol between each cut so infection does not transfer.
  • Discard safely: Bag infected leaves in trash. Never compost diseased plant parts!
🌿

Step 2: Home & Organic Remedies (Safe & Low Cost)

🌿 Student Neem Oil Recipe:

Mix 2 teaspoons of pure cold-pressed Neem Oil + 1/2 teaspoon of mild dish soap into 1 Liter of lukewarm water. Shake well and spray both top and underside of leaves every 7 days in the early evening.

🧂 Baking Soda Mildew Spray:

Mix 1 tablespoon of baking soda + 1 teaspoon vegetable oil + 1 drop dish soap in 1 gallon (3.8L) water. Creates an alkaline barrier that blocks fungal spores.

🏪

Step 3: Garden Store Eco-Treatment

  • Liquid Copper Fungicide: Apply according to package directions during dry overcast weather to stop spore germination.
  • Bio-fungicide (Bacillus subtilis): Friendly soil bacteria spray that colonizes leaf surfaces and outcompetes harmful fungi naturally.
🛡️

Step 4: Prevention & Future Plant Care

  • Water at the roots only: Keep leaf surfaces completely dry. Use drip watering or water early in the morning so sun dries splashes.
  • Mulch around stem: Add 2 inches of clean straw or wood chips to prevent soil pathogens from splashing onto lower leaves.
  • Crop Rotation: Do not plant tomatoes, potatoes, or eggplants in the same soil bed for 2 consecutive seasons.

1 Liter Neem Oil Spray Recipe

Student Tested • Plant Safe
Active Ingredient (Neem Oil) 2.0 Teaspoons (10 ml)
Emulsifier (Mild Dish Soap) 0.5 Teaspoon (2.5 ml)
Water Base (Warm / Luke-warm) 1.0 Liter (1000 ml)
Application Frequency Every 5-7 days (Sunset)
How to mix:
  1. Add warm water first so the natural neem oil doesn't solidify.
  2. Add the dish soap to the oil first, then stir gently into the water until milky white.
  3. Test spray on 1 leaf 24 hours prior to treating the whole plant.
  4. Always spray in the late afternoon or cloudy weather to prevent sun leaf scorch.

💬 Ask BioShield

Common Plant Disease Questions

Simple, straightforward answers to the most common questions students and gardeners ask us.

Can I still eat fruits/vegetables from a blighted plant? ▾

Yes, with care! If the fruit itself looks firm, smooth, and free of dark lesions or rot, it is generally safe to wash thoroughly, peel, cook, and consume. However, never eat fruit that has active rot, mold, or mushy spots, and never can (preserve) tomatoes from blighted vines because blight lowers acidity.

Why shouldn't I compost infected leaves? ▾

Most home compost bins do not reach temperatures above 140°F (60°C) needed to kill fungal spores like Alternaria or Phytophthora. If you compost diseased leaves, the spores survive the winter and will re-infect your garden next spring through the compost!

What is the golden rule of watering plants to prevent disease? ▾

Water the roots, never the leaves! Fungal and bacterial spores need free-standing moisture on a leaf for 4 to 8 hours to germinate and penetrate plant cells. Water at soil level using a watering can or drip hose in the morning so any accidental splashes evaporate quickly in the daytime sun.

How does Neem Oil work and is it safe for bees? ▾

Neem oil comes from the seeds of the neem tree (Azadirachta indica). It coats fungal spores to smother them and contains azadirachtin which disrupts pest feeding. It is safe for beneficial earthworms and birds. To protect bees, always spray in the evening after bees have returned to their hives, and never spray directly onto open flowers.
